## Abstract

Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells are a form of adoptive T-cell therapy widely investigated for the treatment of cancer. While viral transfection methods are the gold standard to generate CAR T cells, non-viral CRISPR-engineering using electroporation can create a more precise and homogenic form of gene-editing. This is accomplished by specifically cutting the DNA using a CRISPR-associated protein 9 (Cas9) and knocking-in a CAR transgene by stimulating the homology directed repair (HDR) mechanism with an HDR template. Moreover, non-viral CRISPR-engineering is faster, safer and cheaper than viral methods. Non-viral techniques for CAR T-cell engineering open a new and promising world in gene editing and immune therapy, with several early phase clinical trials in the pipeline.

## Internship Assay

As part of the thesis internship at the Laboratory of Experimental Hematology (University of Antwerp), an experimental assay was conducted using the **2D3 cell line** — a TCRαβ-deficient, CD8αβ and NFAT-EGFP stably-transfected T cell line derived from acute leukemia.

**Objective:** Evaluate the expansion and non-specific activation potential of the 2D3 cell line using PMA and ionomycin stimulation, with EGFP expression as a readout of T cell activation via flow cytometry.

**Key findings:**
- Viability remained stable throughout culture (~86% mean)
- PMA/ionomycin stimulation drove EGFP expression to 89.75% after 24h (p < 0.0001)
- Unstimulated controls remained below 0.4% EGFP expression
- The 2D3 cell line was confirmed as a versatile and cost-effective model for CAR/TCR transgene screening
